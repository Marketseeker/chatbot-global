Contractor website not getting leads? Check these 8 things first.

By Chatbot Global · 22 November 2026 · 8 minute read

Quick answer

If your contractor website gets visitors but no calls, the problem is usually on the page, not in Google. Check eight things on your own phone: tap-to-call, a clear first screen, one next step per page, load speed, the words you use, visible proof, a way to ask questions after hours, and basic tracking. Most fixes take an afternoon.

We build website chatbots for service companies in 20 trades. This week we ran this exact checklist on our own site, and it caught a missing sitemap, links that went nowhere and a page that took more than six seconds to show its main content in a phone speed test. So this list comes with fresh grass stains.

Your analytics say people are visiting. Your phone disagrees. Usually that gap comes down to a handful of small things on the page, and you can find them yourself in about ten minutes with the phone in your pocket.

1. Can a visitor call you with one tap?

They should. On a phone, your number needs to be a tap-to-call link near the top of every page, not plain text tucked into the footer.

Test it now. Open your site on your phone and tap the number. If your phone offers to copy the text instead of dialing, that's the fix. A number that lives only in the footer is playing hide and seek, and customers don't play for long.

2. Does your first screen say what you do, where, and how to reach you?

A visitor should know all three before scrolling. What do you do? Do you work in my area? How do I reach you? Those are the three things every visitor needs to know before they'll call.

"Quality you can trust since 1998" answers none of them, though it does let people know you've been around. "Emergency plumbing in North Dallas, call or text 24/7" answers all three in nine words.

Try this: screenshot your first screen, show it to a friend for five seconds, then take the phone back. Ask what you do and where. If they hesitate, rewrite the headline.

3. Is there one clear next step on every page?

Every page needs one obvious thing to do next. Call, request a quote or start a chat. Pick the main one for that page and make it the biggest button on the screen.

Contractor Magazine found the same pattern on sites that weren't bringing in work: the call to action buried in the middle of the text, a phone number that wasn't click-to-call, and no form at all.

The opposite problem is real too. Five buttons that all shout at once is a menu, not a next step.

4. Does your site load fast on a phone?

Aim for under three seconds on a mobile connection. That's a common best-practice target, and slower pages lose visitors before they see a thing.

Test it with Google's free PageSpeed Insights. Paste in your home page, choose the mobile results, and start with the biggest items on its list. The usual suspects are oversized photos straight off a phone camera and scripts for tools nobody uses anymore.

Every tool you add to a website is another passenger in the truck. Some of them earn the seat. Check that each one does.

5. Do your pages use the words your customers search?

Write the way customers talk, not the way the trade talks. Customers type "AC blowing warm air," not "refrigerant charge diagnostics."

Contractor Magazine gives the example of a plumbing company whose pages talked about a product brand name instead of the plain words people actually search for. Google can only send you people whose words match yours.

A quick way to find the right words: write down five ways customers describe their problem when they call you. Then check that each one appears on your site, ideally in a heading.

6. Can visitors see proof they can trust you?

Show real reviews and real work, close to the top. A few named reviews with dates, photos of your own jobs and your license details, if your state requires one, do more than any slogan.

Specific beats general. A review with a name, a date and the job you did says more than five gold stars and nothing else.

Skip the stock photo of a model in a spotless hard hat pointing at a clipboard. Nobody has ever hired him. Your crew on a real job, even on a cloudy day, beats him every time.

7. What happens when someone has a question at 9pm?

If the only way to reach you is a phone you can't answer, evening visitors usually leave. Give them a way to reach you that works when you're on a job or off the clock.

Speed matters here. Research published in Harvard Business Review found that most companies are not responding to online inquiries nearly fast enough. A message that sits until tomorrow afternoon is competing with whoever replied tonight.

You have three common options:

  • A form with an instant reply, so the visitor knows it landed and when you'll call.
  • A website chatbot that answers common questions right away and emails you the visitor's details.
  • An answering service, if most of your work comes by phone rather than through the website.

8. Can you see what visitors actually do?

Without tracking, every fix is a guess. Three free tools cover most of what a service company needs:

  • Google Search Console shows the searches that brought people to your site.
  • Google Analytics shows how many people visited and which pages they saw.
  • Microsoft Clarity shows where people tap and how far they scroll.

Guessing is a fine way to pick a lunch spot. It's an expensive way to run a website.

What we found when we ran this on our own site

On 20 November we ran a full audit of chatbotglobal.com, and it was humbling in a useful way. Here's what it turned up:

  1. No sitemap and no robots file. Google had to discover our pages on its own. We added both the next day.
  2. Footer links that went nowhere. On one page, the Privacy and Terms links pointed back to the same page. Fixed.
  3. A page title too long to show in full in Google results. Shortened.
  4. Phone speed scores of 71 and 76 out of 100 on our two main pages. The carpet cleaning page took more than six seconds to show its main content in the phone test. The biggest extra weight was our analytics tag, our own chat widget and our styling script.

The first three took an afternoon. The speed work is still on our list, and yes, the chat script we offer is part of that weight. That's exactly why check 4 is on this list: every tool on a page should earn its place, ours included.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my website or my marketing is the problem?+

Look at Google Search Console or Google Analytics. If people visit but don't call or fill in a form, the website is the problem. If almost nobody visits, the problem is being found, and the fixes are different.

How fast should a contractor website load on a phone?+

Under three seconds on a mobile connection is the common best-practice target. Test your home page with Google's free PageSpeed Insights and fix the biggest items it lists first.

Do I need a new website to get more leads?+

Usually not. Tap-to-call, a clear first screen, one button per page, real reviews and a way to reach you after hours can all be added to the site you already have.

Should my website have a form, a phone number or a chat?+

A tap-to-call phone number on every page comes first. Then add at least one way to reach you when you can't pick up, such as a form or a chat, so evening visitors have somewhere to go.

Does a chatbot help a contractor website get leads?+

It can help visitors who have a question when you can't answer, by replying right away and sending you their details. It won't fix a slow site or a hidden phone number, so do those checks first.
